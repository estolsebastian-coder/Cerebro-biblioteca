Perhaps a more signiﬁcant demonstration of Villard’s
detailed knowledge of new carpentry methods is his rendering of a system for bracing the spokes of a hoisting wheel
(23r). Villard explains this in the inscription: “In this manner one braces a wheel without cutting the axle” (Figure 9,
upper right). What Villard is describing is one of the singular advances made in the construction of windlasses during
the Gothic era. The structural integrity of these wheels was

bracing methods that did not require the cutting, and consequently the weakening, of the axle. The solution was to
construct crane wheels with thicker, squared axles, around

the straddling spoke arms to grip and twist the axle in the
manner of pliers on a square nut. Villard does not render
the rim of the wheel, seeming particularly fascinated with
the method of bracing the spokes to the axle. Each brace is
