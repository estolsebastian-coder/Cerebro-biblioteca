and related construction drawings, Method of Measurement as well as relevant general
instructions and guidelines, including valid Uruguay’s laws and Uruguay’s and local

2 EARTHWORK

2.1 Excavation

2.1.1 Foundations and Pits (option)
The existing soil shall be excavated first to the general excavation level shown in the
excavation drawings. Excavated material approved for use as backfill shall be stored
in a place indicated by the Purchaser. Excavated material in excess of that required for
backfill will be stockpiled to the landfill area shown by Purchaser.
The excavation shall be extended at least 1.0 m outside the footings. Underlaying soil
below the general excavation level shall be compacted with heavy (5 t) mechanical
tampers and backfilled with compacted sand to the right level. The bearing modulus
after compaction shall be at least E1 = 50 MPa and E2 / E1  2.2 measured with plate
bearing tests. The number of tests shall be at least 1 per 500 m2. The density shall be
\>95 % compared to the density obtained with modified Proctor Compaction Test.

If required compression of natural soil can not be achieved in connection with lack of
continuous grading (uniform grading soil), than 500 mm thick soil layer shall be
removed under each footing and backfilled by granular material with the uniformity
coefficient U>5. That layer shall be thoroughly compacted in 200-300 thick layers by
mechanical tampers. The bearing modulus after compaction shall be at least E1 = 50
MPa and E2 / E1  2.2. The number of tests shall be at least 1 per 500 m2. The density
shall be >95 % compared to the density obtained with modified Proctor Compaction
Test.

Suitability of compacted underlaying soil for piling works shall be specified after
general excavation with the Purchaser and Designer. If the bearing capacity of
underlaying soil is not adequate enough for the piling works, subgrade surface will be
strengthened with layer of crushed stone, thickness 500 mm (= ´piling platform´).

Layer of crushed stone shall be compacted by trucks and grading machines in

After the piling works the places of deeper foundations under general excavation level
shall be excavated to the right level as shown in the excavation drawings. Under laying
soil below the excavation level shall be compacted with heavy (5 t) mechanical
tampers and backfilled with compacted sand to the right level. The bearing modulus

\>95 % compared to the density obtained with modified Proctor Compaction Test.

If existing soil contains wood, humus, roots etc., at the bottom level of excavation, all
that kind of material shall be replaced with compacted sand.

To prevent soil disturbance excavations shall be maintained free of water during the
construction works. Dewatering can be carried out by pumping from sumps  800
mm with a distance of ca. 50 m around the excavation, reaching at least 0.5 m below
the bottom of the excavation. Drainage water shall be conducted to the ditch or to the
main storm water pipeline in the mill area.

2.4 Filling and Levelling
Geotextile (filter fabric, weight > 180 g/m2, strength >7 kN/m) shall be installed on
the bottom of the excavation where bottom is on fine cohesive soil layer.

The excavations shall be backfilled with compacted filling material. Backfilling
material shall be uniform graded gravel or crushed stones with particle size from 0.5
mm to 65 mm. Alternatively, excavated soil suitable for backfilling as a granular,
suitably graded materials required for the specified compaction without any organic
materials can be used. Particle size distribution curve of backfilling material shall be
located between limits shown in picture below.

Backfilling material – limits for particle size distribution curve

Under footings shall the gravel be compacted to the density of plate bearing value E1
\> 50 MN/m2 and E2/E1  2.2.

In the area at suspended slab shall the gravel be compacted to the density of plate
bearing value E1 > 45 MN/m2 and E2/E1  2.2. In the Pulp Storage area shall the
gravel be compacted to the density of plate bearing value of E1  50 MN/m2 and E2/E1
 2.2.

Filling outside the external walls shall be made in 200 mm thick horizontal layers.
Each layer shall be compacted with a vibrating plate. The density of plate bearing
value of E1 > 40 MN/m2 and E2/E1  2.2 shall be reached.

The thickness of the layers to be compacted must not exceed 200 mm. One plate
bearing test per each 500 cubic meters is required, however, one test per 600 square
meters of floor area in minimum. The density shall be >95 % compared to the density
obtained with modified Proctor Compaction Test.

3 INSITU REINFORCED CONCRETE

3.1 General

3.1.1 Details, Drawings and Instructions
All the work must be carried out in accordance with the drawings, sketches, bar
bending schedules, this specification and instructions, which are issued to the
Contractor by the Purchaser at the start and during the course of the Contract. Large-
scale details take precedence over small-scale details. Scaling sizes from the drawings
shall not be permitted and in all cases figured dimensions shall be used. The
Contractor will satisfy himself with all the details before proceeding with the work
and any inaccuracy shall be referred to and cleared with the Purchaser in advance.

Reinforcement drawings and bar schedules issued by the Engineer will only contain
those bar marks that are essential to the structural integrity of the reinforced concrete
members. The design, supply and fixing of any other bar reinforcement required by
the Contractor as support for the structural reinforcement during the casting process,
or spacers between bars shall be the responsibility of the Contractor. There will not be
any bar schedules of ground floor slabs. If rebars are prefabricated outside the site
area, the contractor must also provide bending equipment to the site and
reasonable amount of extra rebars for late changes and additions.

3.1.2 Inspection before Concreting
The Contractor shall give notice as required by the Purchaser before each concrete
pour so that an inspection can be made before the concrete is placed.

3.1.3 Defective Work
The Contractor shall make good, at his own expense and to the satisfaction of the
Purchaser, all defects arising, in the opinion of the Purchaser, from work or materials
not in accordance with the drawings or Specification, which appear during the work
and after completion of the work within the maintenance period.

Damaged formwork shall not be reused if, in the opinion of the Purchaser, the making
good would impair the surface appearance of the concrete.

Any finished work, which the Purchaser judges to be below the standard of the
accepted sample or to be different in appearance or colour from parts of the works
already constructed, or which is stained or damaged, will be classified as defective
work.

Where for any reason the casting of a part of the work is interrupted before the
designed position of a construction joint is reached and the intended appearance of the
concrete is thereby spoilt, that part of the work would be treated as defective work.

3.2 Materials

3.2.1 General
The Contractor shall ensure that all materials conform to the current Uruguay’s
standards and are to the satisfaction of the Purchaser. The Purchaser's agreement must

delivery made to site.

Once the Contractor has been given the Purchaser's written consent for the source,
quality, mixing plant and Contractor of the materials for concreting purposes, these
shall be adhered to throughout the works.

The Contractor shall supply the Purchaser with certificates showing compliance with
the above.

3.2.2 Storing
Cement, aggregate, water and reinforcement are to be stored on site so that no
contamination or deterioration will occur. Cement shall be kept in a dry state and used
in sequence of delivery to site. Cement shall be stored in the dry and clear of the
ground.

All materials which are unidentifiable or do not comply with this specification shall be
removed from the site immediately at the Contractor's expense.

3.2.3 Cement
The cements used shall be in accordance with the requirements of Uruguay’s
Standards. The cement shall be normally setting ordinary Portland cement grade
complying with the standard UNIT 020:03, UNIT 1014:96, UNIT 1085:03, unless
otherwise specified on the drawings. In some places, Sulphate resistant cement is
used..

A cement manufacturer's certificate shall be made available to the Purchaser on
request.

Concrete in exposed members shall be of the same cement type.

3.2.4 Aggregates
Aggregates shall be in accordance with the requirements of Uruguay’s Standards.

3.2.5 Reinforcement
The reinforcement steel (GG500) shall be in accordance with the requirements of
Uruguay’s Standards.

3.3 Concrete Mixes

3.3.1 General
The Contractor shall employ competent and experienced personnel on the site of the
works, who shall firstly supervise all stages in the preparation and placing of the
concrete. They shall supervise the mixing of concrete, carry out site tests and the
casting of works test specimen. Concreting shall only be permitted after the
requirements of Section 3.5 Sampling and Testing of this Specification have been met
with respect to aggregate tests and preliminary strength tests.

Prior to work commencing on site the Contractor shall prepare and test proposed
sample mixes for the Purchaser together with his proposals for weights of fine and
coarse aggregates before site works are started. Water/Cement ratio shall at all times
be kept to a minimum to produce concrete of the workability and strength required.
The Contractor shall obtain the Purchaser's consent to the water/cement ratio he
intends to adopt and maintain at all times during the course of the work, allowance
being made for the moisture content of the aggregates. The Contractor shall not be
absolved of the responsibility of producing the specified concrete strength even after
acceptable materials have been used.

3.3.2 Structural Concrete
The structural concrete shall be in accordance with the requirements of Uruguay’s
Standards. The concrete shall be chosen considering especially the importance of the
final structure (durability, environmental conditions etc.), execution of work and
climatic conditions.

The quality and strength class of the concrete shall be defined in the structural
drawings. The concrete strength class is marked as characteristic cylinder compressive

3.4 Quality of Work

3.4.1 General

3.4.12 Curing
All surfaces of structural concrete shall be protected from loss of moisture during the
curing period, which shall be the first four days after casting for concrete using
Ordinary Portland Cement and the first three days after casting for concrete using
Rapid-Hardening Portland Cement. Formwork left in position will be regarded as

The concrete may be treated by applying a curing agent approved by the Purchaser.
The agent should be coloured in such a way that the continuity and uniformity of the

easily removable from the finished surface.

All concrete surfaces shall be cured by means of either of the following methods,
depending on the location of the particular pour:
a. By covering it as soon as practicable after casting with a layer of sand, Hessian or
other approved material kept constantly wet throughout the curing period, or by
other methods approved by the Purchaser.
b. By thoroughly wetting it and covering it with an approved plastic membrane kept
in contact with the surface throughout the curing period, or by other methods
approved by the Purchaser.

3.4.13 Concreting in Cold Weather

At temperatures below +2 C or when night frosts may be expected, the mixing water,
sand and aggregates shall be heated by acceptable means to ensure the concrete has a
temperature of not less than +5 C at time of placing. It shall be maintained above +2
C for a minimum period of 3 days or until the concrete has hardened whichever
period is greater by means of artificial heating together with suitable protection.

Materials used in mixing the concrete and all contact surfaces shall be free from ice
and snow.

Concrete surfaces in an exposed location shall be protected for three days or for such
period as the Purchaser directs by means of tarpaulin paper or tarred felt supported not
less than 25 mm above the concrete surface.

instructed by the Purchaser. Where it is possible to maintain the air temperature above

Purchaser's agreement.

temperature of the air and the materials.

3.6.2 Concrete Quality

3.6.2.1 Materials
Materials used for concrete floors shall comply with Clause 3.3.2 Structural Concrete.

Special attention must be paid to the amount of fine aggregate. In concrete floor
constructions the amount of fine aggregate with particle size below 0.125 mm
together with cement may not exceed 400 kg/m3.

The maximum grain size should be at least 16 mm for slab thickness less than 100
mm, and at least 20 mm for slab thickness equal to or in excess of 100 mm. The
amount of grain size over 8 mm should be 50…55 % from the total amount of
aggregate. In 30-40 mm toppings the maximum grain size should not exceed 8 mm. In
order to get dense concrete the aggregate grains should be slightly round formed.

3.6.2.2 Concrete Mixes
Concrete used for concrete floors shall be structural concrete complying with Clause
3.3.2 Structural Concrete.

Consistence of concrete mix shall be chosen so that quality demands, compaction
methods and transportation to the final target of usage have been considered. In order
to minimize cracking and deformations of the floor surface it is essential to keep the
amount of water as low as possible. However the water/cement ratio should not
exceed 0.40.

It is possible to actuate to the quality of concrete mixes by plasticizers. For this
purpose water-reducing agent should be used instead of cement-reducing one. For
workability of the mix the compatibility of plasticizer with cement must be established
beforehand. Heavily plastiziced concrete mix segregates easily which must be catered
when planning transporting, storing before casting and composition of concrete mix.
Heavily plastiziced mix may replastizice also after hardening when starting troweling.
In that case it may be difficult to get an even floor surface.

Prior to commencing the work the Contractor shall present a clarification of the
concrete mix to be used for the Purchaser’s approval.

3.6.3 Preparations prior to the Topping
To assure the bond of the topping the base concrete slab must be cleaned carefully
prior casting the topping. A pressurized water jet should be used; brushing alone
doesn’t remove dust and debris. The surface shall be kept moistened at least 24 hours
prior to casting the topping. When putting the topping in place there shall not be any
loose water on the surface of the base concrete slab.

3.6.5 Finishing Concrete Floors

3.6.5.1 Concreting
The transportation of fresh concrete must be planned so that there will be no
segregation.

To improve the bond between the topping and the base concrete slab a 1:1 cement-
sand slurry or a neat cement paste, each of a creamy consistency, shall be brushed into
the surface of the base slab (toppings  60mm). The topping must be placed
immediately (before the slurry dries out).

The bond into the base slab requires very thorough vibration of the topping.

The temperature of the concreting space as well as the base slab should be at least +10
C. The concreting space should be kept in the same unchanged temperature. To
ensure the hardening of the surface of the topping possible exhaust gas of heating
devices must be led away.

3.6.5.2 Finishing and curing
Troweling should not be done until after the moisture film and sheen have disappeared
from the floated surface and the concrete has hardened enough to prevent an excess of
fine material and water from being worked to the surface. Too early troweling reduces
abrasion resistance. It should be noted that some additives affect to the starting time of
hardening.

The first troweling is performed with the board to straighten the surface. The edges are
troweled manually with a steel float. Following trowelings are done with the power-
troweling machine equipped with float blades. It is required at least three passes of
power troweling to ensure abrasion resistance. Time must be allowed between each
troweling for the concrete to stiffen and the water sheen to disappear. The tilt of the
trowel blades should be increased with each pass to exert additional pressure as the
concrete hardens. Each successive troweling should be made in a direction at right
angles to the previous pass.

To prevent shrinkage cracking the evaporation of concrete moisture must be prevented
right after the troweling. This can be done by using plastic sheeting or spraying a
liquid-membrane-forming curing compound on the finished surface. If using the latter
system it must be clarified prior to commencing the work the means of removing the
membrane and the compatibility with floor coatings.

The temperature on the surface of the floor must be at least +5 C during the hole
curing time. The curing time must be at least 14 days.

details.
