THE MECHANICS OF THE PALMA CATHEDRAL (MALLORCA):
EQUILIBRIUM ANALYSIS

SANTIAGO HUERTA 1

ABSTRACT

The Palma Cathedral is an extraordinary building. The main nave is 20 m wide and 44 m high, but
its most astonishing feature is the solution of a seemingly impossible problem. The lateral naves
are 9 m span and are supported by slender pillars 22 m high. How is this possible? Already in 1912,
Rubio Bellver, a disciple of Gaudi, made an excellent static analysis of the cathedral. However, he
used an approximate geometry. We now have a detailed survey, which provides some surprising
results: some pillars, for example, are about 0.40 m out of plumb. The same values are found in the
outer buttresses. Examination of the extrados shows that there are huge masses of ashlar masonry
above the keystones of the main vaults and the transverse arches of the main nave. Some local
aspects also deserve attention: the flying buttresses are also deformed and show slippage on top,
etc. There is no doubt that the cathedral is stable because it has resisted for more than six centuries,
but an explanation of the stability taking into account these deformations, seems relevant. The
theoretical framework would be the modern Limit Analysis of Masonry Structures which validates
the "old" equilibrium approach. This is what I am working on and what I would like to report on
at this conference.
